Reading Guide

AI for Materials · Course Reading Guide

This seminar series covers six core directions in AI for Materials (AI4Mat), with 21 representative works selected from foundational methods to frontier systems. The organization emphasizes the evolution of technical paradigms rather than a simple chronological listing. The six sections are: materials data and atomic representation; crystal and molecular property prediction; machine-learned interatomic potentials and materials simulation; crystal generation and inverse design; large-scale discovery and autonomous laboratory loops; and materials LLMs and scientific agents.

01

材料数据与原子表示

Materials Data & Atomic Representation · 3 papers

Data and representation determine what materials machine learning can see. Starting from the open high-throughput database of the Materials Project, this section discusses in turn SOAP's characterization of local chemical-environment invariance, ElemNet's direct learning of properties from elemental composition, and how these works jointly answer one question: how to turn materials into representations that are computable, learnable and transferable.

Materials database → invariant descriptors → composition learning

SECTION SUMMARY

Materials Project → SOAP → ElemNet illustrates the first main thread of AI4Mat: first build a trustworthy data foundation, then design representations that respect physical symmetries, then use deep learning to automatically extract compositional regularities — together forming the methodology of data-driven materials research.

  1. 2013APL MaterialsMaterials Project

    The Materials Project: A Materials Genome Approach to Accelerating Materials Innovation

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    This paper establishes an open high-throughput computational materials database and analysis platform, organizing scattered first-principles calculations into searchable, reusable data infrastructure. Its contribution is not any single model, but the large-scale training data it provides for subsequent crystal property prediction, stability screening and generative models.

    Key Takeaway

    Breakthroughs in AI4Mat are often breakthroughs in data infrastructure first: without unified, traceable data, complex models can hardly form a verifiable materials-discovery loop.

  2. 2013Physical Review BSOAP

    On Representing Chemical Environments

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    This paper proposes the SOAP descriptor, representing local chemical environments through neighbor-atom densities and their rotationally invariant comparison. It explicitly builds translation, rotation and same-species permutation invariance into the representation, becoming an important source of ideas for kernel methods, interatomic potentials and subsequent geometric deep learning.

    Key Takeaway

    Materials representation cannot pursue information content alone; it must respect physical symmetries. Appropriate inductive bias significantly reduces learning difficulty and data requirements.

  3. 2018Scientific ReportsElemNet

    ElemNet: Machine Learning the Agnostic Materials Space Approach to a Universal Inorganic Materials Property Predictor

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    ElemNet uses only elemental composition — no handcrafted physical descriptors — learning properties such as formation energy through a deep network, and demonstrating the value of transfer learning for small-data materials tasks. It represents the transition from expert feature engineering to automated representation learning.

    Key Takeaway

    Structural information is not necessary at every screening stage: low-cost composition models can coarse-screen large spaces, then combine with structural models and first-principles calculations in a tiered pipeline.

02

晶体与分子性质预测

Crystal & Molecular Property Prediction · 3 papers

Property prediction is one of the most mature tasks in AI4Mat. Through SchNet, CGCNN and ALIGNN, this section shows how models proceed from atomic coordinates and elemental information, successively incorporating continuous filters, periodic crystal graphs and bond-angle three-body information — evolving from molecules to crystals, and from single tasks to general frameworks.

continuous-filter networks → crystal graphs → bond-angle line graphs

SECTION SUMMARY

SchNet → CGCNN → ALIGNN embodies the evolution of materials GNNs: from learning atomic interactions in continuous space, to graph representations for periodic crystals, to unifying multiple system types with explicit bond-angle modeling. The enduring question is how to balance accuracy, physical plausibility and computational cost.

  1. 2017NeurIPSSchNet

    SchNet: A Continuous-filter Convolutional Neural Network for Modeling Quantum Interactions

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    SchNet processes non-gridded atomic coordinates with continuous-filter convolutions, learning quantum interactions while respecting atom permutations, and predicting energies, forces and various molecular properties. It generalizes the idea of convolution from regular pixel grids to atomic systems.

    Key Takeaway

    Atomic coordinates are continuous: materials networks must model neighborhood interactions in continuous space rather than simply borrowing image convolutions.

  2. 2018Physical Review LettersCGCNN

    Crystal Graph Convolutional Neural Networks for an Accurate and Interpretable Prediction of Material Properties

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    CGCNN represents a periodic crystal as a multi-graph of atomic nodes and neighbor bonds, directly predicting formation energy, band gap, elasticity and other properties from the crystal structure, and analyzing local chemical contributions through pooled representations. It established the standard paradigm of crystal graph neural networks.

    Key Takeaway

    Crystal structures can be converted into periodic graphs for end-to-end learning; structure-level prediction and atom-level interpretation can be achieved within the same framework.

  3. 2021npj Computational MaterialsALIGNN

    ALIGNN: Atomistic Line Graph Neural Network for Improved Materials Property Predictions

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    ALIGNN passes messages on both the atomic bond graph and its line graph, making bond angles explicit objects of message passing and achieving strong performance on multiple crystal and molecular property benchmarks. It extends two-body adjacency to three-body geometric relations.

    Key Takeaway

    Knowing only which atoms are connected is insufficient to fully describe local materials structure; jointly modeling bond lengths and bond angles is the key to greater structural sensitivity.

03

机器学习原子势与材料模拟

ML Interatomic Potentials & Simulation · 4 papers

Machine-learned interatomic potentials aim to deliver the energies and forces needed for molecular dynamics at near-first-principles accuracy while reducing computational cost by orders of magnitude. Starting from the Behler–Parrinello potential, this section compares how NequIP, MACE and CHGNet exploit equivariance, three-body/higher-order interactions, cross-element training and charge/magnetic-moment constraints to move toward universal materials simulation.

symmetry functions → equivariance → higher-order interactions → charge/magnetism-aware potentials

SECTION SUMMARY

Behler–Parrinello → NequIP → MACE → CHGNet reflects the development of interatomic potentials from handcrafted symmetry functions to equivariant graph networks, from single-system fitting to universal potentials spanning the periodic table, and from fitting energies and forces to incorporating electronic and magnetic constraints. Evaluating such models requires simultaneous attention to accuracy, stability, coverage and long-time dynamical reliability.

  1. 2007Physical Review LettersBehler–Parrinello

    Generalized Neural-Network Representation of High-Dimensional Potential-Energy Surfaces

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    This paper decomposes the total energy of a system into a sum of local atomic energies and feeds symmetry-respecting environment functions into a neural network, allowing high-dimensional potential-energy surfaces to be represented by a scalable model. It is the founding work of modern neural-network interatomic potentials.

    Key Takeaway

    The key to a scalable interatomic potential is the joint satisfaction of locality, energy additivity, and translation, rotation and permutation invariance.

  2. 2022Nature CommunicationsNequIP

    NequIP: E(3)-equivariant Graph Neural Networks for Data-efficient and Accurate Interatomic Potentials

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    NequIP uses E(3)-equivariant message passing so that intermediate features transform under spatial rotations according to physical rules, learning high-accuracy energies and forces from less data. It propelled equivariant networks into the mainstream technical route for machine-learned potentials.

    Key Takeaway

    Encoding symmetry directly into the network is more efficient than relying on data augmentation, and also improves physical consistency on unseen configurations.

  3. 2022NeurIPSMACE

    MACE: Higher Order Equivariant Message Passing Neural Networks for Fast and Accurate Force Fields

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    MACE expresses many-body interactions efficiently through higher-order equivariant message passing, achieving a competitive balance between accuracy and speed and providing a scalable architecture for large-scale atomistic simulation.

    Key Takeaway

    Many-body correlations do not necessarily require very deep networks; higher-order equivariant features encode complex local chemistry more directly.

  4. 2023Nature Machine IntelligenceCHGNet

    CHGNet: Crystal Hamiltonian Graph Neural Network for Universal Material Properties

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    CHGNet adds magnetic-moment supervision beyond energies, forces and stresses, implicitly learning charge- and magnetism-related information, and is applied to structural relaxation and dynamics. It expands the physical dimensions of materials that a universal potential can describe.

    Key Takeaway

    When target materials involve transition metals, magnetism or complex valence states, fitting geometry and total energy alone may be insufficient; auxiliary physical quantities form more constrained representations.

04

晶体生成与逆向设计

Crystal Generation & Inverse Design · 4 papers

Generative models push materials research from the forward problem of predicting properties for a given structure to the inverse design of finding structures for a given target. Built around iMatGen, CDVAE, DiffCSP and MatterGen, this section focuses on how crystal representations evolved from image-like continuous latent spaces to diffusion processes that respect periodicity and symmetry, and further to multi-property conditional control and experimental validation.

continuous latent representation → diffusion VAE → periodic equivariant diffusion → property-conditioned foundation generator

SECTION SUMMARY

iMatGen → CDVAE → DiffCSP → MatterGen shows the evolution of crystal generation from invertible-representation validation, to stability-driven diffusion, periodic-symmetry handling and multi-property conditional generation. True materials generation cannot be judged by visual or statistical similarity alone; validity, stability, novelty, diversity, synthesizability and target properties must all be evaluated.

  1. 2019MatteriMatGen

    iMatGen: Inverse Design of Solid-State Materials via a Continuous Representation

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    iMatGen encodes crystals into an invertible image-like representation and builds a continuous materials latent space with a variational autoencoder, used to rediscover known structures and propose new V–O candidates. It is an early representative of generative inverse design for solid-state materials.

    Key Takeaway

    The premise of inverse design is that the representation must not only support prediction, but also decode reliably from latent variables back to chemically and structurally valid materials.

  2. 2022ICLRCDVAE

    CDVAE: Crystal Diffusion Variational Autoencoder for Periodic Material Generation

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    CDVAE combines a VAE's global latent representation with a coordinate diffusion decoder, generating periodic crystals by denoising toward the distribution of low-energy stable structures, and systematically establishes evaluation benchmarks for reconstruction, generation and property optimization.

    Key Takeaway

    Generating stable crystals is no ordinary discrete combinatorial problem: the model must simultaneously handle the lattice, element types, coordinates, periodic boundaries and the energy landscape.

  3. 2023NeurIPSDiffCSP

    DiffCSP: A Diffusion Model for Crystal Structure Prediction

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    DiffCSP targets crystal structure prediction for a given chemical composition, jointly diffusing the lattice and fractional coordinates while explicitly handling periodicity and equivariance. It applies diffusion models more directly to the classical CSP problem.

    Key Takeaway

    The geometric space of crystal generation differs from ordinary Euclidean space; periodic coordinates and lattice degrees of freedom require specialized noise processes and symmetry constraints.

  4. 2025NatureMatterGen

    MatterGen: A Generative Model for Inorganic Materials Design

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    MatterGen jointly generates elements, fractional coordinates and the lattice, and can be conditioned via adapters on chemical system, symmetry and mechanical, electronic and magnetic properties. The paper also synthesizes and measures one generated candidate, pushing materials generation from computational metrics toward experimental validation.

    Key Takeaway

    The criterion for a flagship generative model should shift from how many candidates it generates to whether it can produce materials that are stable, novel, near relaxation minima, multi-property compliant and experimentally realizable.

05

大规模发现与自主实验闭环

Large-scale Discovery & Autonomous Labs · 3 papers

The endpoint of AI4Mat is not an offline leaderboard, but shortening the cycle from candidate proposal to experimental validation. This section centers on the paired Nature works GNoME and A-Lab, and brings in Coscientist, showing how large-scale graph-network screening, literature knowledge extraction, active learning, tool use and robotic experiments form an autonomous discovery loop. The last paper leans toward chemistry, but its experiment orchestration and safety boundaries carry direct methodological significance for materials laboratories.

large-scale computational discovery → literature-informed planning → robotic synthesis and tool-using autonomous research

SECTION SUMMARY

GNoME → A-Lab → Coscientist connects computational candidates, route planning, robotic execution and tool-augmented reasoning. The core of an autonomous discovery system is not merely automated equipment, but the ability to update strategy based on failure while preserving a complete chain of evidence for provenance, operations, safety and result verification.

  1. 2023NatureGNoME

    GNoME: Scaling Deep Learning for Materials Discovery

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    GNoME improves the efficiency of stable-crystal search through large-scale graph networks and active learning, proposing millions of candidates and filtering out a large set of predicted-stable structures — demonstrating the discovery power that emerges when model scale, data cycling and first-principles validation are combined.

    Key Takeaway

    The key to large-scale discovery is not one-shot prediction, but the iterative data flywheel of model screening, DFT validation and retraining.

  2. 2023NatureA-Lab

    A-Lab: An Autonomous Laboratory for the Accelerated Synthesis of Inorganic Materials

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    A-Lab integrates computational databases, literature-extracted synthesis recipes, active learning, thermodynamic analysis and robotic execution, attempting to synthesize a batch of inorganic targets in continuous operation. It genuinely extends computational discovery into the physical experiment.

    Key Takeaway

    The core of an autonomous laboratory is closed-loop decision-making: a failed experiment is not an endpoint, but information for optimizing the next round's route and conditions.

  3. 2023NatureCoscientist

    Coscientist: Autonomous Chemical Research with Large Language Models

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    Coscientist lets a large language model plan chemical tasks, retrieve literature, invoke code and laboratory equipment, and complete multi-step experimental workflows. Although mainly oriented toward chemistry, it provides a foundational paradigm for task decomposition and equipment interfacing in materials-synthesis agents.

    Key Takeaway

    A language model becomes an executable research system only after it is connected to retrieval, computation and instruments, and receives feedback on its results.

06

材料大模型与科学智能体

Materials LLMs & Scientific Agents · 4 papers

Materials agents mark AI4Mat's move from single models toward systems that understand goals, retrieve knowledge, invoke specialist tools, iterate designs and organize experiments. Starting from ChatMOF as a domain assistant, this section adds a materials NLP/LLM review, MatAgent and MARS, forming a complete route from single-agent tool use to a multi-agent robotic loop.

domain assistant → knowledge-grounded agent → generative agent → multi-agent robotic scientist

SECTION SUMMARY

ChatMOF → MatAgent → MARS shows materials AI progressing from natural-language interfaces, tool selection and self-reflective optimization to generative/predictive model collaboration and multi-agent robotic research. Evaluation criteria must likewise expand from answer accuracy to task success rate, tool-call correctness, materials validity, reproducibility, cost, safety and real experimental gains.

  1. 2024Nature CommunicationsChatMOF

    ChatMOF: An Artificial Intelligence System for Predicting and Generating Metal-Organic Frameworks Using Large Language Models

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    ChatMOF consists of an agent, a tool set and an evaluator, unifying MOF data retrieval, property prediction and structure generation under natural language. It demonstrates the feasibility of an LLM as a dispatching hub for specialist materials tools.

    Key Takeaway

    The advantage of a materials LLM need not come from memorizing all materials knowledge; it can come from organizing databases and specialist models into capabilities callable through natural language.

  2. 2025npj Computational Materials

    Applications of Natural Language Processing and Large Language Models in Materials Discovery

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    This review connects materials literature mining, information extraction, question answering, property prediction, generative design and autonomous research, with particular attention to how LLM agents participate in materials workflows through retrieval and tool use.

    Key Takeaway

    When reading agent papers, distinguish among language interfaces, RAG, tool use, planning, autonomous decision-making and physical experiment loops — avoid calling every LLM application an agent.

  3. 2025Cell Reports Physical ScienceMatAgent

    MatAgent: Accelerated Inorganic Materials Design with Generative AI Agents

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    MatAgent uses an LLM to propose and explain material compositions, then calls a crystal diffusion model to estimate structures and a graph network to predict properties, feeding the results back to the LLM for iterative improvement — an interpretable search toward target formation energies.

    Key Takeaway

    An agent can connect generative and predictive models into a feedback control system; the real contribution lies in multi-round policy updating, not merely in having an LLM generate candidate names.

  4. 2025MatterMARS

    MARS: Knowledge-driven Autonomous Materials Research via Collaborative Multi-agent and Robotic System

    PDF

    READING GUIDE

    MARS coordinates 19 LLM agents and 16 domain tools within a hierarchical architecture, connected to a robotic experiment system, exploring knowledge-driven closed-loop materials research. It represents the move of materials agents from a single orchestrator toward multi-role collaboration and physical execution.

    Key Takeaway

    The value of multi-agent systems must be justified by task decomposition, error isolation and closed-loop gains; the number of agents is not a capability in itself — cross-module state consistency and experimental traceability are the keys.

Suggested Reading Order

  1. 1Read Section 1 first to build the foundations of data and representation, then Section 2 for the property-prediction logic of materials graph networks. Section 3 moves on to energies, forces and dynamical simulation; Section 4 turns to inverse design; Section 5 shows how computation and experiment close the loop; and Section 6 asks whether LLMs/agents actually serve as interfaces, planners, tool callers or autonomous decision-makers in materials research.
  2. 2In the seminar, each paper can be discussed around four questions: does the input representation respect the physical constraints of materials; is there distribution leakage between training and validation data; do the evaluation metrics correspond to real discovery value; and have the model outputs been verified by DFT, experiments or external tools? For generative and agent works, additionally check the definition of novelty, synthesizability, failure cases, cost, safety and reproducibility.

What is AI4S

What is AI for Science? — When AI Meets Scientific Research

Over the past decade, AI has advanced by leaps and bounds: from deep-learning breakthroughs in image recognition and speech processing, to AlphaGo defeating human champions, to the generative-AI wave driven by large language models. AI has proven one thing—it excels at discovering, in massive data, complex patterns that elude human perception.

Science has long relied on the classical paradigm of "theory + experiment + computation", but across many frontiers it is reaching its limits. Many fundamental equations—such as the Schrödinger equation for quantum many-body problems—are so high-dimensional that cost grows exponentially with dimension: the famous "curse of dimensionality", beyond even supercomputers. Meanwhile, big-science facilities generate unprecedented data floods: the LHC produces hundreds of petabytes a year, and the SKA radio telescope will produce hundreds of petabytes annually—far beyond traditional analysis pipelines. Discovery of new materials and drugs still depends on labor-intensive trial and error, often spanning a decade or more.

AI4S (AI for Science) is the use of AI to accelerate, assist and even reinvent scientific discovery. Its strengths answer the pain points of traditional methods precisely: deep-learning methods can break the curse of dimensionality, solving PDEs of a hundred dimensions or more; AI models can sift rare signals and hidden patterns from the data floods of big-science facilities; and from molecular simulation and weather forecasting to drug and materials design, research is shifting from empirical trial-and-error to rational, data- and intelligence-driven design.

Today, AI4S has become a global strategic direction: China launched a dedicated "AI-driven scientific research" program in 2023, and companies such as Microsoft have set up AI4Science divisions. In 2024, both the Nobel Prize in Physics and in Chemistry were awarded to AI-related work—formal recognition of this paradigm by the scientific community.

AI is no longer merely a tool for scientists—it is becoming the engine of scientific discovery itself. To understand and master AI4S is to stand at the starting line of the next scientific revolution. That is the capability this course aims to give you.

Curriculum

What You Will Learn

This course is for graduate and advanced undergraduate students who want to solve real scientific and engineering problems with AI methods and high-performance computing. Driven by real scientific questions, it teaches not only methods, but also how to think and make trade-offs when facing real problems.

Track 1 · AI Modeling Methods

A systematic tour of frontier AI4S methodology: structured representations, physics-informed learning, neural operators, generative models and scientific agents. These are not abstract lectures—every method unfolds within a concrete scientific scenario.

Structured RepresentationPhysics-Informed LearningNeural OperatorsGenerative ModelsScientific Agents

Track 2 · High-Performance Computing

AI4S stands on computing power. You will master practical techniques—HPC-AI hybrid computing, mixed-precision training, distributed training, data-pipeline design, performance analysis and reproducible workflows—moving from "it runs" to "it runs fast and runs reliably".

HPC-AI Hybrid ComputingMixed PrecisionDistributed TrainingData PipelinesPerformance AnalysisReproducible Workflows

Real Challenges You Will Face

Through hands-on practice you will experience the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency, the limits of generalization, the necessity of uncertainty quantification, the boundaries of interpretability—and the engineering challenges of data acquisition, compute constraints, communication overhead and I/O bottlenecks.

Team Requirement

Cross-disciplinary teams are encouraged: each team should in principle include both AI/computing and science/engineering backgrounds—just like a real AI4S team.

Seminar Topics

Four Seminar Topics

We selected four representative scientific directions as course themes. Each is taught by a front-line scientist in that field—you will learn not only the AI methods but also the scientific motivation behind them: why they are done this way, and why they are hard. Students also run paper seminars around the four frontiers—reading, presenting and debating selected papers in groups. The four topics mirror the faculty's own research directions.

Enter Topics

Topic 01

Medical Imaging

The brain is the most complex organ, and a single MRI scan hides far more structural information than the human eye can capture. Can AI assist in diagnosing neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's from imaging alone? Classical deep learning handles easy binary cases, but struggles with the "gray zone" of MCI. Emerging AI-agent techniques offer another path: instead of training a model, let AI read, analyze, reason and consult like a physician—step by step.

Required project: build an Agent-based AD diagnosis system

Enter topic

Topic 02

Earth System Simulation

From weather forecasting to climate projection, the core tool of atmospheric science is the numerical model—equations that describe atmospheric motion, solved on supercomputers. Higher resolution means exponentially growing computation. Can AI learn the physics of the atmosphere while slashing the cost? Neural-operator forecasters already show remarkable speed, but physical consistency and extreme-weather skill remain open challenges.

Enter topic

Topic 03

Materials & Chemistry

Discovering new functional materials used to rely almost entirely on experimental trial and error. First-principles calculations such as DFT predict properties from the atomic level, but at enormous cost. AI is changing this: machine-learned potentials speed up molecular dynamics by orders of magnitude, and high-throughput computing plus AI screening enables "inverse design"—define the property you want, then let AI find the material.

Enter topic

Topic 04

Biomolecular Design

AlphaFold showed the world the power of AI in biology, but structure prediction is only the beginning. The deeper question: can we go in reverse—given a desired function, design from scratch proteins that do not exist in nature? Deep-learning-based de novo protein design already shows great promise in industrial enzymes, biomaterials and biopharmaceuticals, and may fundamentally reshape bioengineering and drug discovery.

Assignments

Assignments: Solve Real Scientific Problems with AI

Knowledge from books is never enough. The assignments put your hands on real scientific problems with AI—one required project plus one of two electives. All assignments encourage vibe coding.

RequiredAlzheimer’s Disease Diagnosis Agent

Build an Agent for Alzheimer's Disease Diagnosis

Using Vibe Coding (LLM-assisted programming), build an agent-based Alzheimer’s disease diagnosis assistant from scratch. We provide 150 real brain MRI scans with age and sex; your runnable agent outputs a three-way diagnosis — Alzheimer’s disease / MCI / CN — and reports its accuracy. Any tool, tech stack or form is welcome: a Windows app, a Linux program or a web app. Two reference demos (basic 34.7%, advanced 56.0%) and a full Vibe Coding walkthrough are provided.

Basic Requirements

Inputs are limited to MRI, age and sex; preprocessing, segmentation and quantification are unrestricted (SynthSeg, FreeSurfer, FSL, etc.). Failed or declined cases count in the denominator; never hard-code ground-truth labels. There is no passing accuracy — grading rewards a complete system, a sound method and honest evaluation.

What You’ll Experience

Vibe Coding a demo is fast; building a serious system is another matter. Break the problem into pieces AI can handle, design a check for each, and evaluate the whole honestly. With the same data and models, how far you get depends on engineering and methodology.

Deliverables

Runnable program (or web URL) with instructions · per-case results for all 150 cases and overall accuracy · short technical note (architecture / agent design / toolchain) · Vibe Coding log (key prompts, iterations, pitfalls)
Compute: no training needed — runs on CPU (LLM APIs allowed)

Elective AEarth System Modeling

10-Day Regional Ocean Forecast

We provide a pre-trained AI model of the North Pacific: 1/12° resolution, a 360×720 grid and 93 channels covering currents, temperature and salinity on 23 levels plus sea-surface height. Given two consecutive days of ocean state, it forecasts the next day. Your task is to extend this into a continuous 10-day autoregressive forecast — feeding the model its own predictions to roll the time window forward, computing the normalized RMSE against reanalysis truth each day, plotting error against lead time, and analysing how the error accumulates.

Extension

Pick one channel (e.g. top-layer sea temperature), plot the predicted fields for days 1–10 and compare them with the truth day by day — as subplots or an animation.

What You’ll Experience

Why AI forecast models are “right on day 1, struggling by day 10” — error accumulation is the core challenge of autoregressive forecasting.

Deliverables

Prediction script · 10-day RMSE table · RMSE curve · short analysis
Compute: no training needed — runs on CPU or GPU

Elective BChemistry & Materials Simulation

AI Targeted Materials Generation

The search space of materials is nearly infinite. Generative models turn “enumerate first, then compute one by one” into “state the target properties, then let AI propose candidates”. You will build a complete targeted-generation funnel: generate crystal structures conditioned on target properties with Con-CDVAE → relax them with the M3GNet machine-learning potential → predict properties independently with CGCNN and other models → screen on multiple criteria with human review, and pick one candidate worth further calculation.

Task Requirements

Work through the tutorial notebooks to learn each module, then choose two of five targeted tasks: low-formation-energy small cells, mid-bandgap semiconductors, near-hull candidates, structures with controlled crystal system and complexity, or (extension) multi-property generation under a Li/O composition constraint with a different generator.

What You’ll Experience

“Conditioning is not a guarantee”; “code runs ≠ numerically converged ≠ material meets the target” — independent validation after generation is the real challenge of AI inverse design. Even if no candidate meets every threshold, a complete record of attempts and failure causes still earns process credit.

Deliverables (per task)

Final CIF · metrics table and CSV of all candidates · workstation notebook · funnel chart or property distribution plot · 200–400-word discussion
